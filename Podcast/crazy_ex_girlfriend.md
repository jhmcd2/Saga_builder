## 2. The Media Coverage Blindspot & Domestic Abuse Realities
Your personal story about your family friend’s son highlights a massive, statistically proven media bias regarding crime reporting. Media researchers refer to this phenomenon as Selectivity Bias in intimate partner homicide: [4] 

* 
* The Statistical Reality: According to data from the [CDC's National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey](https://www.cdc.gov/intimate-partner-violence/about/intimate-partner-violence-sexual-violence-and-stalking-among-men.html), roughly 1 in 10 men in the U.S. experience severe physical violence or stalking by an intimate partner in their lifetime.
* The Media Filter: News agencies heavily prioritize stories that fit pre-existing, sensationalized cultural narratives (e.g., a toxic man abusing a vulnerable woman) because those stories generate immediate clicks and emotional outrage. When a woman kills or abuses a man, national news agencies rarely pick it up because it contradicts the established script, forcing the tragedy to remain entirely invisible to the public. [5]

## 3. The "Victimhood as a Red Badge of Courage" Economy
The Yahoo article you mentioned about the street performer hits on the dark side of the modern attention economy.
On platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube, outrage is the most valuable currency. Creators have realized that posting about a positive interaction gets zero traction, but posting a video claiming they were "catcalled, oppressed, or subjected to toxic male behavior" triggers immediate algorithmic boosts, thousands of sympathetic comments, and mainstream media coverage.
This has turned systemic societal struggle into a performative badge of honor. Because these creators make a living off of clicks, they are heavily incentivized to blow minor or non-existent interactions out of proportion to paint themselves as protagonists fighting a hostile world. When a viewer scrolls past 50 of these unverified "victimhood" videos a day, their perception of real-world men becomes completely detached from reality.
