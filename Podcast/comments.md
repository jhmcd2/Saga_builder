You have perfectly deconstructed the 2017 Justice League (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F010fq514&q=Justice+League) disaster. It is one of the most fascinating messes in Hollywood history because, as you noted, it wasn't a case of "good guys vs. bad guys." It was a toxic collision of a desperate studio, an impossible structural mandate, a director with outdated, abusive methods, and a cast that felt completely betrayed.
Here is how both sides were simultaneously right and wrong in that situation:

1. Joss Whedon (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01vz80y&q=Joss+Whedon) & The Studio (Warner Bros.)

• Where they were "right" (or practical): Warner Bros. was terrified of the dark, polarizing reception of Batman v Superman. Zack Snyder's original assembly cut of Justice League was massive, sprawling, and fundamentally clashed with the lighter, crowd-pleasing tone the studio desperately wanted. Whedon (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01vz80y&q=Whedon) was hired precisely because of his track record with The Avengers (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F062zm5h&q=The+Avengers)—he was a proven script-doctor who knew how to aggressively strip away subplots (just like he did with Captain America's deleted arc in 2012) to meet a strict, mandate-enforced 2-hour runtime. From a corporate standpoint, he was brought in to perform emergency triage on a multi-million dollar asset.
• Where they were 100% wrong: Whedon (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01vz80y&q=Whedon) allegedly imported a toxic, combative "90s-style auteur" ego to the set. Multiple cast members, including Ray Fisher (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0xn4p75&q=Ray+Fisher) (Cyborg) and Gal Gadot (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F08ygdy&q=Gal+Gadot) (Wonder Woman), reported that he was dismissive, verbally abusive, and went so far as to threaten Gadot's career when she questioned changes to her character. Instead of acknowledging that he was a guest director finishing another man's grieving project (Snyder left due to a family tragedy), Whedon (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01vz80y&q=Whedon) reportedly demanded total, unquestioned submission to his rewrite.

2. The Cast & The Characters

• Where they were "right": The actors had spent months building a specific, serialized vision with Zack Snyder (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0852vk&q=Zack+Snyder). Ray Fisher (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0xn4p75&q=Ray+Fisher), in particular, had been told by Snyder (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0852vk&q=Snyder) that Cyborg was the "heart of the movie." When Whedon (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01vz80y&q=Whedon) arrived and completely gutted Cyborg's backstory, reduced his role, and replaced heavy emotional beats with corny, forced jokes (like Flash accidentally falling on Wonder Woman's chest), the actors felt the integrity of their work was being cheapened. They weren't just fighting for their screen time; they were fighting against what they saw as regressive, lazy writing.
• Where they made things worse: The cast went into the reshoots with palpable resentment, creating an instantly combative environment. While their loyalty to Snyder (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0852vk&q=Snyder) was admirable, film production is ultimately a business, and the studio owns the project. Refusing to cooperate with a corporate-mandated pivot—even a poorly executed one—escalated a difficult situation into an outright war. Henry Cavill's infamous contractually obligated Mission: Impossible mustache, which had to be clumsily CGI'd off his face, became the perfect visual metaphor for how uncooperative and compromised the entire production had become.
Ultimately, the theatrical cut of Justice League failed because you cannot seamlessly graft the DNA of Marvel's The Avengers onto the structural skeleton of Zack Snyder's DC universe. Whedon (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01vz80y&q=Whedon) was given a broken, mathematically impossible equation to solve, and his toxic management style ensured that the cast wouldn't help him solve it.
